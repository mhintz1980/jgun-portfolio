# Recommended drivetrain camera direction

**Keep a straight rail parallel to the gearbox, but aim obliquely across the active parts.** Camera and target move together with a fixed offset. Keep the lens stable through the rail and preserve the pacing Mark currently likes. No continuous orbit or animated yaw is required.

NotebookLM's corrected concept is this fixed oblique rail, with an unrendered 75 mm radial / 75 mm axial example (45 degrees off-axis). Astra agrees with the concept and recommends testing **about 60 degrees off-axis** instead, within a 55–65-degree range. One coherent starting pose is **100 mm total radial offset, 57.7 mm axial lead**, on the existing output-side sign; standoff is115.5mm.

Why it should read better: the current rail is already about2.4inches off-axis, but the long axial lead keeps the view only16degrees from looking straight down the gearbox. Reducing that axial bias exposes the sides of the carriers, bearing gaps and relative depth rather than stacking the parts visually end-on.

This is a candidate, not a fitted or approved camera. Its close distance may need adjustment for actual installed CAD and desktop/narrow framing. Entry/exit must match position, target, up/orientation, lens and derivatives inside the existing timing windows. NotebookLM's assertion that a fixed rail automatically guarantees C1 joins is rejected.

## Review of the proposed motion

Preserve the owner's sequence: zoom toward the smooth black Ring Switch; fade the surrounding assembly into darkness; reveal raw aluminium and rapid rotation; approach and contact with the scissor knurling tool; traverse for roughly one or two seconds; retract; restore black finish while the rest of the tool returns.

Recommendations for developing that sequence, separate from owner requirements:

1. Keep the same ring silhouette, bore, shoulders, and smooth edge lands through every material transition. Only the intended OD band receives the knurl.
2. Make the developing texture follow the roller contact and axial traverse. A whole-ring texture change at once would hide the causal action that makes this concept effective.
3. Choose a view that shows the contact, the remaining smooth band, and the completed band together. Keep enough visible roller motion to explain the tool even while the ring spins rapidly.
4. Establish the ring's support, spin axis, and tool clearance. If the visual uses an isolated floating part, explicitly treat that as a presentation choice when developing the scene, rather than silently claiming a real machining setup.
5. Let the rollers separate from the OD before the tool exits. Decelerate the ring before the assembly returns so the final pose reads as the finished switch seated in the tool.
6. Retain the diamond relief when the surface returns to black, and hold the completed finish briefly under grazing light.

Only the traverse has an owner-specified duration. The old study's ten-second runtime does not constrain this proposal. Black → aluminium → black remains a cinematic transition between manufacture and finish; it does not depict an anodizing operation.
